Week 6 Discussion — Does the Cemetery Sensor Run Hot?

Carry out a one-sample hypothesis test and separate statistical evidence from causal explanation.

How this discussion works

Solve, present, then respond

Work the problem below before reading classmates’ solutions. In your initial post, show enough statistical work that another student can follow your method, then state the result in the language of the situation. Post your solution in the class discussion board; this page itself does not submit responses.

A clear table, calculation, labeled graph, calculator output, or photo of neatly organized handwritten work is fine when appropriate. The goal is statistical communication, not extra prose.

Case file

The problem

Suppose a paranormal-investigation group uses a particular environmental sensor because it has been carefully calibrated in the laboratory. Under a standard background condition, the manufacturer and the group’s own calibration work indicate that the sensor has mean output μ₀ = 50.0 units with population standard deviation σ = 8.0 units.

Before interpreting any unusual location, the investigators need a baseline. The number 50.0 is therefore not just an arbitrary comparison value; it represents what the sensor is expected to average when operating under the stated calibration condition.

The group then places the same model sensor in an old cemetery and collects 64 readings at fixed intervals. Assume for this exercise that the readings are sufficiently independent. Their sample mean is x̄ = 52.3 units. A difference of 2.3 units might be meaningful, or it might be the kind of difference that ordinary sampling variation produces even when the true mean is still 50.0.

A hypothesis test formalizes that question. We temporarily suppose that the cemetery environment has the same population mean as the calibration environment and ask how unusual a sample mean of 52.3 would be under that assumption. Use a two-sided test with α = 0.05 because a shift in either direction would be of interest.

The statistical question: Is the observed sample mean difficult to explain using the calibration model alone, and if it is, what exactly have we learned about the sensor readings—as opposed to the cause of those readings?

Initial post

What to include

  1. State H₀ and Hₐ in symbols.
  2. Compute the standard error and the z test statistic.
  3. Find the two-sided p-value.
  4. Make the reject/fail-to-reject decision at α = 0.05 and write the statistical conclusion in context.
  5. Explain why a statistically detectable shift in sensor output would not, by itself, identify the cause of the shift.
Replies

Engage with classmates’ statistics

Verify a classmate’s z statistic or p-value. Then inspect their final sentence: does it distinguish evidence that the mean sensor output changed from a claim about why it changed?

A useful reply checks a calculation, compares methods or assumptions, or asks a statistically specific question. Avoid replies that only say that you agree.
