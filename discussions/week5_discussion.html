Week 5 Discussion — How Many Images Remain Unexplained?

Use a random sample to estimate a percentage, and explain what a 95% confidence interval tells us about that estimate.

The situation

Trail cameras collect thousands of photographs of wildlife and other activity in forests. Some pictures are easy to identify. Others are blurry, partly blocked, or affected by insects close to the lens, fog, or reflections. Even after careful review, the source of some images may remain unknown.

Imagine a research team with an archive of thousands of photographs from similar forest sites. The team wants to estimate what percentage of the entire archive would remain unexplained after each image goes through the same review process.

Rather than review every photograph, the team randomly selects 200 images. After review, 34 are still classified as unexplained. The remaining 166 have been identified or otherwise accounted for.

The sample provides an estimate of the percentage in the whole archive. But a different random sample of 200 images might have a slightly different result. This is why we use a confidence interval: it gives a range of plausible values for the unknown population percentage, taking sampling variation into account.

A 95% confidence level describes the method. If we repeatedly took random samples and made an interval from each one in the same way, about 95% of those intervals would contain the true population percentage. We will use that method to make one interval from this sample.

Your initial post

Use the 200 images and the 34 unexplained results to answer the questions below. Show your calculations and explain your conclusions in your own words. You may use a calculator and round your final percentages to one decimal place.

  1. Find the sample proportion. Divide the number of unexplained images by the total number reviewed:
    \(\displaystyle \hat p=\frac{\text{number unexplained}}{\text{number sampled}}\)
    What percentage of the sample remained unexplained?
  2. Estimate the uncertainty. The standard error estimates how much a sample proportion tends to vary from one random sample to another. Calculate:
    \(\displaystyle SE=\sqrt{\frac{\hat p(1-\hat p)}{n}}\qquad(n=200)\)
    For an approximate 95% confidence interval, find the margin of error using \(\displaystyle ME=1.96(SE)\).
  3. Build and interpret the interval. Find its lower and upper endpoints:
    \(\displaystyle \hat p-ME\quad\text{to}\quad\hat p+ME\)
    Convert both endpoints to percentages. Then write a sentence explaining what your 95% confidence interval suggests about all comparable images in the archive that would remain unexplained under the same review procedure.
  4. Think about the result. What can the team reasonably conclude from its interval? What can it not conclude about the causes of the unexplained photographs? If the team reviewed a larger random sample, how would you expect the uncertainty in its estimate to change?

Respond to a classmate

Read a classmate’s solution and respond to the reasoning behind it. You might compare your interval endpoints, comment on whether the interpretation refers to the whole archive rather than just the sample, or discuss how reviewing more images could affect the margin of error.

Include at least one specific observation or question about their statistical reasoning so the conversation helps both of you make sense of confidence intervals.
