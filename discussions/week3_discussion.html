Case file

The problem

Investigators who monitor allegedly haunted locations often bring environmental instruments with them: temperature sensors, audio recorders, electromagnetic-field meters, and similar devices. A number flashing on a screen can feel dramatic in the moment, especially if it appears at the same time as an unusual sound or experience. Statistics gives us a way to ask a calmer first question: is the measurement actually unusual compared with ordinary background variation?

Suppose an investigation team places an electromagnetic-field sensor in the hallway of an old hotel. Before the investigation, the same sensor is used on several quiet calibration nights at the same location. Those background readings are approximately normally distributed with mean 2.4 mG and standard deviation 0.5 mG. We will treat that normal distribution as the team’s model for ordinary readings at this location.

At 1:17 a.m. on the investigation night, one investigator reports hearing a knock from an apparently empty room. Almost immediately afterward, the sensor records 3.7 mG. The timing makes the reading interesting, but timing alone does not tell us how unusual 3.7 mG is or what caused it.

A z-score lets us translate the raw reading into a standardized distance from the background mean. Once we know that distance, the normal model lets us estimate how often a reading at least that large would occur under the stated background conditions.

The statistical question: How unusual is 3.7 mG under the calibration model, and what conclusions are—and are not—justified by finding that a measurement is statistically unusual?

Initial post

What to include

  1. Compute the z-score of the 3.7 mG reading.
  2. Use the normal model to estimate the probability of a calibration reading at least this large.
  3. Translate the z-score into ordinary language: how many standard deviations above the calibration mean is the observation?
  4. Would the statement “this reading is unusual under the calibration model” be statistically justified? Would the stronger statement “this reading proves paranormal activity” be justified by this calculation? Explain the difference.
  5. Name one feature of the calibration process you would want to inspect before trusting the normal model.
