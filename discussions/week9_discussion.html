Case file

The problem

This discussion uses the same Q1 2026 fireball case studied in the module. The trajectory dataset contains 194 baseline events and 67 events from 2026. For reports_count, the baseline mean is about 83.572 reports per event and the 2026 mean is about 125.269.

At the threshold reports_count ≥ 50, 40 of the 67 2026 events qualify. The two-sided comparison used in the case file gives a p-value of about 0.0429. The report counts are strongly right-skewed.

Your task is to decide what these numbers actually support—and what tempting headlines go beyond them.

Initial post

What to include

  1. Compute the difference in mean reports_count, 2026 minus baseline.
  2. Explain why strong right-skew makes the mean sensitive to a few very highly reported events. What additional summary would you want beside the mean?
  3. Interpret p ≈ 0.0429 in the context of the threshold comparison. Do not describe it as the probability that the null hypothesis is true.
  4. The threshold 50 was chosen by the analyst. Explain how changing the threshold could change the proportion comparison and why threshold sensitivity should be reported.
  5. Write a one-sentence headline that is accurate about the variable reports_count. Then write one plausible but statistically unjustified headline and explain exactly what it gets wrong.
Replies

Engage with classmates’ statistics

Choose a classmate whose cautious headline differs from yours. Decide whether their wording refers to reporting intensity, event frequency, or something else, and use the dataset definitions to explain whether the wording is supported.

A useful reply checks a calculation, compares methods or assumptions, or asks a statistically specific question. Avoid replies that only say that you agree.
