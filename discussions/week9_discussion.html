Week 9 Discussion — Can a Track Predict a Stride?

Use footprint and stride measurements to fit a line, make a prediction, and assess its accuracy.

Discussion instructions

Predicting stride length

You'll work with seven paired measurements of footprint length and stride length. Study the scatterplot, fit a regression line, and use it to make a prediction. A residual and R-squared will help you judge how closely the line describes the measurements.

Show your calculations and explain what the results mean in centimeters. Submit your work on the class discussion board; this page contains the assignment prompt but does not submit your response.

Case file

Footprints and strides

Footprint length and stride length describe different features of a trackway. Stride length is the distance between successive corresponding footprints; it can be affected by an animal's size, gait, speed, and the ground beneath it. Longer footprints may therefore be associated with longer strides, although we should not expect an exact rule.

A field team could use a scatterplot of these measurements to see whether a line would help predict stride length from footprint length. For this exercise, use the following fabricated observations. These numbers do not establish the identity or existence of an unusual animal. Let \(x\) be footprint length and \(y\) be stride length, in centimeters.

x28303234363840
y112118123132136145149

A least-squares line can summarize the relationship and predict a stride length for a footprint within the observed range. Its residuals show how far individual strides differ from the predictions.

The statistical question: How well does a linear model summarize the relationship between footprint length and stride length in this dataset, and what would be unsafe to conclude or predict from that model?

Initial post

What the data tell us

Use the seven pairs above. The Regression Explorer or calculator can help with the arithmetic, but include the equation and your interpretation in your post.

  1. Describe the data. What do footprint length and stride length measure? Which will you use to predict the other? Describe the direction and approximate shape of the scatterplot before computing a line.
  2. Fit the line. Use the seven pairs to find the least-squares equation. Explain in one or two sentences what its slope says in centimeters of stride per centimeter of footprint. How does a least-squares line decide where to go?
  3. Make a prediction. Predict the stride length for a footprint 35 cm long. Is this inside the observed range? What is the difference between that prediction and an actual recorded stride?
  4. Find a residual. For the observed pair \(x=34\), \(y=132\), calculate the residual as observed minus predicted. Does this point fall above or below the line?
  5. Evaluate the fit. Interpret \(r^2\) as a reduction in squared prediction error compared with the mean-only prediction. Explain why a strong linear relationship doesn't prove what animal made the tracks or make predictions far outside 28–40 cm trustworthy.
