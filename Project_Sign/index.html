Briefing

A digitized government progress report has arrived in the course archive. Your statistical review team has been asked to examine the incident table before anyone draws a dramatic conclusion from it.

The file is historically interesting, but the statistical assignment is narrower. You will not determine what the reported objects were. You will determine how recorded counts, times, altitudes, dates, and categories can be summarized and modeled—and where the evidence stops.

Unit of analysis

In these activities, one table row is usually treated as one report entry. That convention is useful for computation, but it does not guarantee that every row represents an independent physical event.

Investigation ledger

These are the principal numerical findings carried forward from one case file to the next. They describe the course dataset under the stated conventions.

Object-count distribution

Among 108 exact counts, \(P(X=1)=0.7037\). The empirical mean is \(2.213\) objects and the population standard deviation is \(3.028\) objects. The distribution has a long right tail.

Time and altitude models

The 78 exact times fall into six-hour blocks with counts \(4,16,35,23\), rather than the uniform prediction of \(19.5\) per block. The fitted altitude normal model assigns probability to impossible negative values, so its limitations must be acknowledged.

Sample estimates

A fixed sample of 36 counts has \(\bar x=1.944\) and \(\hat p=0.6944\). Its 95% intervals contain the known empirical mean \(2.213\) and proportion \(0.7037\).

One-sample decisions

The mean test against \(H_0:\mu=2\) gives \(p\approx0.9123\), so \(H_0\) is not rejected. The right-tailed proportion test against \(H_0:p=0.50\) gives \(p\approx0.0098\), so the null is rejected at \(\alpha=0.05\).

Earlier and later periods

The earlier mean is \(4.161\) and the later mean is \(1.465\). Single-object proportions are \(0.4516\) earlier and \(0.7887\) later. Both procedures describe a shift toward smaller recorded counts in the later period.

Linear trend

The fitted line is approximately \(\hat y=4.613-0.00973x\), with \(r=-0.258\) and \(r^2=0.0667\). Date has a weak negative linear association with count and explains only about 6.7% of its variation.

How to read the evidence

What the calculations can do

  • Describe patterns in the recorded table.
  • Compare stated mathematical models with observed summaries.
  • Demonstrate sampling distributions, intervals, tests, and regression.
  • Identify shifts, outliers, and model failures.

What the calculations cannot do

  • Show how often aerial phenomena occur in the world.
  • Establish that table entries are independent events.
  • Determine the cause or identity of a reported object.
  • Turn association or statistical significance into causation.

Working rule: A statistical conclusion should always name the data, the model, and the limits of the inference.
