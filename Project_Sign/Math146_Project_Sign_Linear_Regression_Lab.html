Statistical briefing

The analysis uses the 102 Project Sign entries that have both an exact calendar date and an exact whole-number object count.

  • \(x=\) days since January 1, 1947
  • \(y=\) number of objects reported
Interpret carefully: A regression line describes association in these recorded entries. It does not show that time caused the reports to change.

Objectives

  • Describe direction, form, strength, and outliers in a scatterplot.
  • Compute a least-squares regression line from summary statistics.
  • Interpret the slope, correlation, and coefficient of determination.
  • Make a prediction and calculate a residual.
  • Recognize weak fit, influential observations, and extrapolation.

Reference formulas

\[ b=r\frac{s_y}{s_x},\qquad a=\bar y-b\bar x,\qquad \hat y=a+bx \] \[ e=y-\hat y,\qquad r^2=\text{proportion of variability in }y\text{ explained by the linear model} \]

Part 1. Examine the paired data

Unlike the two-period comparison, regression retains the ordering and spacing of every date. The scatterplot must be examined before the line is trusted.

Part 2. Compute the regression line

Least squares chooses the line that minimizes the sum of squared vertical residuals. The formula produces a line even when the relationship is weak.

Use the summary statistics below.

\(n\)\(\bar x\)\(s_x\)\(\bar y\)\(s_y\)\(r\)
102239.44182.7502.2843.116-0.2583

\[ b=(-0.2583)\frac{3.116}{82.750}=-0.00973, \qquad a=2.284-(-0.00973)(239.441)=4.6132. \] Therefore, \[ \hat y=4.6132 - 0.00973x. \] Also, \(r=-0.2583\) and \(r^2=0.0667\).

Part 3. Interpret the model

The slope describes average linear change per day. Correlation describes direction and strength, while \(r^2\) records the fraction of variation accounted for by the line.

The slope is \(-0.00973\) object per day, or about \(-0.292\) object per 30 days. The model explains \(6.67\%\) of the variation in recorded object count.

Part 4. Prediction and residual

A residual is the model’s local error for one observation. Large residuals reveal entries that the fitted line does not describe well.

One September 8, 1947 entry has \(x=250\) and reports \(y=12\) objects.

\[ \hat y(250)=4.6132 - 0.00973(250)=2.182, \] \[ e=12-2.182=9.818. \] At \(x=300\), \(\hat y=1.695\).

Part 5. Limits of the model

A fitted line should be audited for weak association, influential observations, impossible predictions, and extrapolation beyond the observed date range.

The observed \(x\)-values range from \(15\) to \(375\). The fitted line predicts the following at \(x=500\):

\[ \hat y(500)=-0.250, \qquad 0=4.6132 - 0.00973x \Rightarrow x\approx474.3. \] Both calculations expose the danger of extending the line beyond the observed data.

Is using the line at \(x=500\) appropriate?

Outlier sensitivity

Two entries on June 29 report 18 objects. Removing only those two points changes the fitted line to

\[ \hat y=3.6546 - 0.00700x,\qquad r^2=0.0703. \]
