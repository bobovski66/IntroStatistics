How surprising is our sample?

The coffee machine

A machine claims to pour 8 ounces on average. Its population standard deviation is known to be 0.5 ounce. Suppose individual fills are normally distributed. Can the sample convince us that the machine is underfilling?

Try this: Move the observed mean closer to 8. Then hold the mean still and increase the sample size. Finally change α. What changes—and what stays the same?

Model: independent, normally distributed individual fill amounts with known population standard deviation σ = 0.5 ounce. The sampling distribution of the sample mean is therefore normal with standard error 0.5/√n. This is a left-tailed z-test for a population mean. The simulation samples from that null distribution; it does not resample the observed data.
