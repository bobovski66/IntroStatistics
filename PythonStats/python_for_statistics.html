The geometric distribution

  
Wait for the first success

  
Text reference: Chapter 2 §2.3 — trials until the first success

  
A geometric random variable counts trials until the first success. For the first success to occur on trial \(k\), failures must occur on the first \(k-1\) trials and a success must occur on trial \(k\).

  
Geometric probability
\(P(X=k)=(1-p)^{k-1}p\). The exponent counts the failures that must precede the first success.

The binomial distribution

  
Count successes in a fixed number of trials

  
Text reference: Chapter 2 §§2.4.1–2.4.3 — successes in fixed trials

  
A binomial random variable counts successes in \(n\) independent trials with constant success probability \(p\). The combination \(\binom{n}{k}\) counts the arrangements of the successes.

  
Binomial probability
\(P(X=k)=\binom{n}{k}p^k(1-p)^{n-k}\). In Python, \(\binom{n}{k}\) is computed by math.comb(n, k).
