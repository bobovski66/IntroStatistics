Case File 3 used annual counts at witness thresholds. This file uses annual counts in regions of the sky. The selection remains limited to events with usable witness-derived trajectory solutions.

Statistical boundary: Witness-report counts are properties of the reporting system as well as the event. They can be affected by brightness and physical scale, but also by population, visibility, weather, geography, media attention, and reporting behavior. Claims about physical causes require evidence beyond this CSV.

Part 3. Annual-count Poisson questions

For each zone, treat the 2021–2025 annual count average as λ and compute the chance of a 2026 count at least as large as observed.

Part 4. The denominator changes the question

A Poisson comparison asks whether the annual number of selected events in a zone is unusual. A two-proportion comparison asks whether the fraction of selected events in the zone changed, given that 2026 has more selected events overall.

A useful contrast: The broad-zone annual count is unusual under the Poisson question, but its share of the selected 2026 events is not significantly different. Both calculations can be correct because they condition on different denominators.

Part 5. Boundary uncertainty

The AMS article states that witness-derived radiants typically carry uncertainties of about 10–20°. A point near a zone boundary may move into or out of the zone under a plausible revision.

Measurement boundary: A small p-value does not reduce coordinate uncertainty. Statistical precision and measurement precision are different problems.
