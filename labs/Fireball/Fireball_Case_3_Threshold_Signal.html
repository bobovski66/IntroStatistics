Question under review

Investigation log

Case File 2 showed that reports_count is heavily skewed. This file avoids averaging individual witness counts and instead counts how many events cross fixed thresholds.

Statistical boundary: Witness-report counts are properties of the reporting system as well as the event. They can be affected by brightness and physical scale, but also by population, visibility, weather, geography, media attention, and reporting behavior. Claims about physical causes require evidence beyond this CSV.

Part 2. Estimate the baseline rates

For each upper threshold, average the eight annual counts from 2018 through 2025. This average is the Poisson rate \(\lambda\) used in the source analysis.

Part 3. Compute exact Poisson upper tails

For an observed count k, calculate \(P(X\ge k)\) under \(X\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda)\). This is the chance of a result at least as large as the Q1 2026 count if the baseline rate remained unchanged.

\[P(X\ge k)=1-\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}e^{-\lambda}\frac{\lambda^i}{i!}\]

Part 4. Check the count model

A Poisson process has variance approximately equal to its mean. The sample variance-to-mean ratio (VMR) across the baseline years is used as a diagnostic.

Why the total is different: The baseline total-event counts vary far more than a Poisson model allows. A tiny Poisson p-value for 2,322 total events would therefore be misleading. The source uses an overdispersed count model for the total and finds that the total is not the central anomaly.
