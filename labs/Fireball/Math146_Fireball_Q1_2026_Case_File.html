Briefing

The American Meteor Society has published an analysis asking whether something changed in the near-Earth meteoroid environment during Q1 2026. Your team has been asked to audit what the public data support.

The assignment is not to decide in advance whether a “swarm” occurred. The evidence will be separated into layers, the selection rules will be documented, and several statistical summaries will be compared. A result that appears dramatic under one statistic may become ordinary under another.

Three evidence layers

Layer A

AMS article

Provides annual Q1 counts from 2011–2026, physical-context claims, selected event descriptions, sonic-boom summaries, and the published Poisson analysis.

Layer B

Radiant CSV

Provides 261 selected events with dates, witness counts, radiant coordinates, trajectory geometry, and start/end coordinates. It does not contain every Q1 event or the article’s sonic-boom fields.

Layer C

Course analyses

Constructs classroom models from the source layers: descriptive summaries, threshold counts, Poisson probabilities, proportion tests, and two-sample inference.

Source claim to audit: The AMS analysis argues that the anomaly is concentrated at the high-witness end of the distribution rather than in the total event count, and that radiant concentrations and physical reports make a purely reporting-based explanation incomplete.

What can and cannot be tested here

Supported by the supplied data

  • Witness-count distributions within the selected events
  • Changes across years and months within stated subsets
  • Counts and proportions inside defined radiant zones
  • Associations among the trajectory variables

Not independently established by the CSV

  • Object mass, composition, or recovered meteorite classification
  • Sonic-boom rates and long-duration report totals
  • Whether reporting behavior changed because of media or AI tools
  • A physical cause for the Q1 2026 pattern
Interpretive rule: A statistical anomaly is a feature of a model and dataset. It becomes a physical explanation only when the measurement process and additional evidence support that step.
