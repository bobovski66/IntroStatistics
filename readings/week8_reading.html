Categorical Models and Chi-Squared Tests

For category counts, evidence is the total mismatch between what a model predicts and what the sample contains.

1. Categorical models have free choices and constraints

Suppose students select one of three lunches: pizza, tacos, or salad. A model lists their probabilities as (p₁, p₂, p₃). Each probability is nonnegative, and together they sum to 1. Choosing p₁ and p₂ determines p₃ = 1 − p₁ − p₂, so only two choices are free.

The corners of the triangle put all probability in one category; interior points mix the three categories. The equal-choice model lies at its center. Observed proportions identify another point, which we compare with the null model.

Check your understanding.

A three-category model has probabilities 0.50 and 0.30 in its first two categories. Find the third.

Show explanation

It is 1 − 0.50 − 0.30 = 0.20.

2. Expected counts convert a probability claim into predictions

Observed counts \(O_i\) are what the sample contains. Under a goodness-of-fit null model with specified probabilities \(p_i\), expected counts are \(E_i=np_i\). They can be nonintegers: an expected count is a model prediction, not an actual count of people.

Worked example.

For 60 students and equally likely pizza, tacos, and salad choices, expected counts are 20, 20, 20. Suppose observed counts are 30, 18, 12. Both totals equal 60; their allocation differs.

Use counts in the calculation, not percentages. Converting 50% to a count requires the sample size: 50% of 60 is 30, while 50% of 600 is 300. The strength of evidence can change with sample size even when percentages match.

3. Chi-squared combines weighted squared gaps

\[\chi^2=\sum_i\frac{(O_i-E_i)^2}{E_i}\]

Subtract to find each gap, square it so opposite signs do not cancel, divide by its expected count to adjust the scale, then add. The same raw gap is more striking against a small expected count than a large one. Every contribution is nonnegative; perfect agreement gives χ² = 0.

Worked example.
LunchOE(O−E)²/E
Pizza30205.0
Tacos18200.2
Salad12203.2

The total is χ² = 8.4. Pizza and salad contribute most, showing where the mismatch lies.

The signed Pearson residual \((O_i-E_i)/\sqrt{E_i}\) shows whether a category is over- or underrepresented. Squaring that residual gives its chi-squared contribution. Contributions alone lose the direction.

Check your understanding.

Why is a gap of 5 more influential when E = 10 than when E = 100?

Show explanation

Its contributions are 25/10 = 2.5 and 25/100 = 0.25. The formula weights the gap relative to the expected count.

4. Goodness of fit: one variable, a specified distribution

State H₀: the population probabilities equal the claimed distribution. State Hₐ: at least one probability differs. Compute expected counts and check independent observations with mutually exclusive, exhaustive categories. For the course procedure, require every expected count to be at least 5.

With k categories and probabilities specified in advance, the degrees of freedom are k−1 because the total count is fixed. If model parameters were estimated from these same data, the degrees of freedom need additional adjustment.

Worked example.

For the lunch example, df = 3 − 1 = 2. The 5% chi-squared critical value is 5.991. Since 8.4 exceeds it, reject the equal-choice model at α = 0.05. A table gives 0.01 < p-value < 0.05.

The result indicates a departure somewhere in the distribution, rather than a separate statistically significant finding for every category. Inspect the counts and contributions to explain the overall result.

5. Independence: two categorical variables in one table

A two-way table records combinations of two categorical variables. Independence means knowing the first category does not change the probability distribution of the second. Under H₀, the variables are independent; under Hₐ, they are associated.

\[E_{ij}=\frac{(\text{row }i\text{ total})(\text{column }j\text{ total})}{n},\qquad df=(r-1)(c-1)\]

The expected count is the row total multiplied by the overall column proportion. Compute all expected counts, check that each is at least 5, and sum the same weighted squared gaps over every cell.

Worked example.
Prefers onlinePrefers in personTotal
Year 1302050
Year 2203050
Total5050100

All four expected counts are 50×50/100 = 25. Each contribution is 25/25 = 1, giving χ² = 4 with df = 1. Since 4 exceeds the 5% critical value 3.841, reject independence at that level.

The table shows an association between year and preference under the sampling model. It does not show that advancing a year causes a preference to change. Other characteristics or differences in selection may account for the pattern.

Check your understanding.

A 4-row, 3-column table has n = 500. A cell’s row total is 120 and column total is 75. Find E and df.

Show explanation

\(E=120(75)/500=18\) and \(df=(4-1)(3-1)=6\).

6. Read the right-tail curve and critical values

A chi-squared curve is supported on nonnegative values. Its shape depends on degrees of freedom. Greater mismatch moves the statistic right, so these tests use the right tail even when the alternative allows departures in several directions.
